# Research: frevo, manguebeat and coco da Xambá

Research notes on three rhythms for the Batuque shows: names, instruments, patterns, tempo, show outlines and sound sources. Date: 2026-11-17.

The frevo and coco sources are solid. Manguebeat has no published rhythm transcription, so most of
its groove detail is inference.

One point affects every show. Capiba and Chico Science are recent composers, so their melodies are
under copyright. The shows use the real rhythms with original melodic lines. This is not legal
advice.

Tags used below: **[src]** means found in a source. **[inf]** means inference.

## 1. Frevo

### a. Name

- The accepted umbrella term is **Frevo**. It has been a Brazilian heritage Form of Expression
  since 2007 and on the UNESCO list since 2012. IPHAN names three types still in use: **frevo de
  rua**, **frevo de bloco** and **frevo-canção** [src: [IPHAN revalidation opinion 2021](https://www.gov.br/iphan/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/aberta-consulta-publica-sobre-o-cirio-de-nazare-pa-o-frevo-pe-e-a-cachoeira-de-iarauete-am/ParecerTcnico_RevalidaoFrevo.pdf/@@download/file), which cites the INRC led by Carmem Lélis].
  - Frevo de rua is instrumental. Brass and percussion bands play it.
  - Frevo de bloco is played by «pau e corda» orchestras (strings, woodwinds, percussion) with a
    female choir.
  - Frevo-canção is sung. The radio orchestras spread it [src: [UNIRIO/PROEMUS, citing Saldanha's UNICAMP thesis, 2008](https://www.unirio.br/proemus/turma-de-2021-1/AFrevotecaSanfonada.pdf)].
- Frevo de rua has three sub-types [src: [USP course paper](https://edisciplinas.usp.br/pluginfile.php/5827122/mod_resource/content/1/Frevo%20_Caio%2C%20Ellen%2C%20Lorena.pdf), [Revista Fênix](https://revistafenix.pro.br/revistafenix/article/download/1318/1124/3588)]:
  - **coqueiro**: short, high, fast notes, led by the brass;
  - **ventania**: sixteenth-note runs in the saxes and clarinets;
  - **abafo**: long loud notes, played when two clubs meet, so each band can drown out the other.
- The Batuque show follows frevo de rua, because it is the instrumental type.

### b. Instruments

Sources: IPHAN; SIMPOM; [ANPPOM 2019](https://anppom.org.br/anais/anaiscongresso_anppom_2019/5983/public/5983-20723-1-PB.pdf).

- Percussion:
  - **surdo**: bass and marking. It generally plays beat 2 [src: [thatdrumblog](https://thatdrumblog.blogspot.com/2020/05/frevo.html)].
  - **caixa**, also called tarol: continuous syncopated sixteenths, with a roll at the end of each
    phrase.
  - **pandeiro**: a sixteenth-note ostinato.
- **Metais** (trumpets, trombones): the lead line, «question» phrases and stabs.
- **Palhetas** (alto and tenor saxes, clarinets, requinta): «answer» phrases and ventania runs.
- **Tuba or bombardino**: the bass line.
- Flute and flautim: high doubling.
- Frevo de bloco only: violão, cavaquinho, bandolim, flute and clarinet.

### c. Patterns, meter and tempo

- A SIMPOM paper calls the named figures «tópicas» [src: [Santos, SIMPOM 2022](https://seer.unirio.br/simpom/article/download/12491/12338/72397)]:
  - **lá e cá**: the metais ask a phrase and the saxes answer.
  - **ventania**: sax sixteenth runs.
  - **Alto da Sé**: the climax, in bars 9 to 12 of each part, on the IV chord.
  - **arrodeio**: a faster chord progression before the part returns to its start.
  - **fotografia**: a rhythmic break in the bridge, where the dancers (passistas) hold poses.
  - **bambeando**: an anticipation on the last eighth note of the bar.
- Form since the 1950s: A (16 bars), a bridge (2 to 8 bars) and B (16 bars), in an ABA shape
  [src: Santos].
- Meter: 2/4, with sixteenth notes as the grid [inf, standard practice].
- Tempo:
  - frevo de rua is «around 120 BPM, often reaching 180 in the street» [src: ANPPOM 2019];
  - a composition plan targets 140 to 160 BPM [src: Santos];
  - frevo-canção is slower than frevo de rua [src: Fênix]. An estimate for it is 125 to 145 BPM
    [inf].

### What makes it special

This is a producer's view. All of it is [inf] except where a source is named.

1. **The surdo hit on 2.** Beat 1 is muted or a ghost note at velocity about 50. Beat 2 is open at
   about 120.
2. **A nervous caixa.** Sixteenths at velocity 70 to 90, with two accent levels. A roll ends every
   phrase of 4 or 8 bars; the roll is [src]. Keep swing at 0 to 5 %. The push comes from
   anticipations, not swing.
3. **Call and response between sections.**
   - Trumpets play the question, staccato at about 40 % gate, velocity 110 to 127.
   - Saxes answer at velocity 90 to 105.
   - The tuba walks the bass on eighth notes.
   - Each section plays in the bar the other section leaves free.
4. **Ventania.** The saxes and clarinets run sixteenths in unison.
5. **Bambeando and fotografia.** The anticipation lands on the last eighth note of the bar. The
   fotografia is a tutti stop and hit.

The melody goes to trumpet 1, with the trombones an octave below. It is an original line.

### d. Show outline

140 BPM. One 2/4 bar is 0.86 s. In the first plan one 16-step wheel was 2 bars; the decision since
then is an 8-step wheel, one bar per turn. Total 120 bars, about 1:43.

| # | Scene | Bars | What enters or changes | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chamada | 4 | Caixa roll, then a tutti hit | The opening signal |
| 2 | Surdo | 8 | Surdo alone | The beat-2 marking |
| 3 | Caixa | 8 | Caixa sixteenths plus a phrase-end roll | The nervous engine |
| 4 | Pandeiro | 8 | Pandeiro | The full rhythm section, enough to dance to [src: Santos] |
| 5 | Tuba | 8 | Tuba walking bass | Low end and harmony |
| 6 | Coqueiro | 16 | Trumpets and trombones, short high stabs | The A part, led by the brass |
| 7 | Lá e cá | 16 | Saxes answer the brass | Call and response |
| 8 | Ventania | 8 | Sax and clarinet runs | The «wind» figure |
| 9 | Bambeando | 8 | Anticipation marked on the wheel | The push |
| 10 | Fotografia | 4 | Rhythmic break and stop | The bridge pose |
| 11 | Abafo | 8 | Long fortissimo brass notes | The clash of two bands |
| 12 | Alto da Sé | 16 | Climax at bars 9 to 12 on IV | Where the peak sits |
| 13 | Finale | 8 | Tutti, caixa roll, final hit | The ending |

### e. Sound sources

- Already in the repository, from Freesound CC0 (see `CREDITS.md`): surdo, caixa and roll.
- Trumpet, trombone, tuba, clarinet and flute: VSCO 2 Community Edition, CC0 [src: [Versilian](https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/)].
- Tenor sax, rope-tension snare and tambourine: VCSL, CC0. The license file was checked at
  [github.com/sgossner/VCSL](https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL).
- Hard to find:
  - a **CC0 alto sax**;
  - brass falls and section stabs, which would have to be built from single notes;
  - a **pandeiro**, probably on Freesound, not checked;
  - **bandolim and cavaquinho**, needed only for frevo de bloco.

## 2. Manguebeat

### a. Name

- **Manguebeat**, also written mangue beat or manguebit. The band calls it simply «mangue».
  «Manguebeat» was the name the press gave it.
- Fred Zero Quatro first used the word in 1992. The manifesto «Caranguejos com Cérebro» is by
  Fred 04 and Renato L., dated 1991 or 1992 [src: [pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manguebeat)].
- Academic uses of the term: Galinsky, *Maracatu Atomico*; Sneed, *Latin American Research Review*
  2019.
- It is a **movement or scene, not one rhythm.** Chico Science fused maracatu, ciranda, embolada and
  coco with rap, hip hop, rock and soul [src: same page].
- The first album of Chico Science & Nação Zumbi, *Da Lama ao Caos* (1994), was produced by Liminha,
  with Chico Neves on samplers [src: [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Lama_ao_Caos)].

### b. Instruments on *Da Lama ao Caos*

Source: the same Wikipedia page.

- **Alfaias** (Gira, Gilmar Bolla 8, Jorge du Peixe): bass and the maracatu baque.
- **Caixa** (Canhoto): an ostinato. There is no drum kit on this album; Pupillo joined later [inf].
- **Percussion and effects** (Toca Ogan): the instruments per track are not documented.
- **Bass** (Dengue): a riff that locks with the main hand of the alfaia [inf].
- **Guitars** (Lúcio Maia): riffs and texture.
- **Samplers** (Chico Neves): dub textures.

### c. Patterns, meter and tempo

- There are no codified named patterns like the maracatu baques. The band borrows baques and cells
  from coco and embolada [inf].
- Meter: 4/4, sixteenth grid [inf].
- Tempo: no published source. The show plan uses about 108 BPM [inf]; measure it on recordings.

### What makes it special

All of this is [inf]. No published transcription was found, and no recording was analysed.

1. **Alfaias with a heavy rock bass.** The bass and the main hand of the alfaia hit together at
   velocity 120. The notes of the answer hand sit at 70 to 90. Swing is about 54 to 56 %, close to
   the maracatu swing the site already measures.
2. **Dub space.**
   - Cut the mids and drop parts out for whole bars.
   - Put a dotted-eighth delay with about 40 % feedback on the caixa and alfaia accents.
   - Use long reverb sends.
   - Leave space on beats 2 and 4 of the bars that drop out.
3. **A coco cell inside the groove.** Use a ganzá or caixa cell in 2 against 4.
4. **A distorted guitar riff as the hook**, doubled by the bass.
5. **Sampler texture**: noise, vinyl and filtered loops. These have to be synthesized; there is no
   instrument to sample.

No melody is required. The guitar riff carries the identity, and it must be an original riff.

### d. Show outline

108 BPM. One 4/4 bar is 2.22 s and one wheel. Total 52 bars, about 1:55.

| 3 | Three alfaias | 4 | Main and answer hands split | The baque inside the band |
| 4 | Caixa | 4 | Caixa ostinato | Drive without a drum kit |

### e. Sound sources

- The alfaia, caixa and agogô are already in the repository, CC0.
- Hard to find: **CC0 electric bass and distorted-guitar multisamples**. The bass can be
  synthesized. Guitar riffs are the gap.
- Delay and reverb are processing, so they need no samples.

## 3. Coco da Xambá

### a. Name

- The term the sources use is **Coco da Xambá**, also Coco de Xambá [src: [Castro, ANPPOM 2018](https://anppom.org.br/anais/anaiscongresso_anppom_2018/5434/public/5434-18196-1-PB.pdf); Revista Continente article on Xambá coco].
- **No source uses «coco de terreiro»** as the accepted name. It reads as a description: a coco from
  the terreiro [inf].
- Coco types vary by place, and the ways of grouping them also vary [src: Journal of Brazilian
  Ethnomusicology, via search].
- Context: Terreiro Santa Bárbara, Ilê Axé Oyá Meguê, at the Quilombo do Portão do Gelo in Olinda.
  The festival is on 29 June.

### b. Instruments

- **Alfaia**, laid on a tied chair and played on **both heads**. This is the Xambá signature: «more
  thunderous, faster» [src: Castro; Revista Continente, citing Carlos Sandroni]. It is the bass and
  the lead.
- **Ganzá**: a continuous ostinato.
- **Pandeiro**: ostinato and fills.
- **Caixa**: drive.
- **Tabica**: named in the sources, not identified.
- **Gonguê** and **agbê** play with the ritual drums [src: Castro]. Agogô and **palmas** also appear
  in Xambá coco on stage [inf].
- **Engomes** (melé, melé-ancó, ian): ritual drums found only in the Xambá [src: Castro].

### c. Patterns, meter and tempo

- One named feature: the coco is built from rhythmic phrases that join into a distinctive final
  **«rufo»**, a roll [src: Castro, citing Alves 2006].
- Its «toque mais vertiginoso» (more dizzying beat) sets it apart from other cocos [src: Cadernos de
  Estudos Sociais 2015, Fundaj, via search].
- Meter: 2/4 [inf].
- Tempo: **no source found.** An estimate is 125 to 145 BPM [inf]. Measure it on recordings.

### What makes it special

All of this is [inf], except the rufo and the two heads.

1. **The two heads of the alfaia talk.** The low head plays the downbeats at velocity 115. The other
   head fills the off-sixteenths at 60 to 85. These are two separate samples.
2. **Ganzá sixteenths** with an accent on the off-beat sixteenth. Swing about 55 to 58 %.
3. **Palmas on the off-beats**, the backbone of the roda.
4. **Phrases that pile up into the rufo.** Build density step by step on the wheel.
5. **The «vertiginous» feel.** The first plan raised the tempo by about 4 to 8 BPM over a scene. The
   decision since then is one tempo per show, so the feel comes from more alfaia notes instead.

### d. Show outline

This is the first plan: 132 BPM, one 2/4 bar is 0.91 s, so one 16-step wheel is 2 bars. Total 112
bars, about 1:42. The script now in use has 12 scenes on an 8-step wheel (see
`expansion-decisions.md`).

| # | Scene | Bars | What enters or changes | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chamada | 4 | Alfaia rufo | The opening signal |
| 2 | Low head | 8 | Alfaia downbeats | The bass |
| 3 | Second head | 8 | Off-beat head | The two-sided Xambá alfaia |
| 4 | Ganzá | 8 | Ganzá | The shaker engine |
| 5 | Pandeiro | 8 | Pandeiro | The weave |
| 6 | Palmas | 8 | Claps | The backbone of the roda |
| 7 | Agogô and agbê | 8 | Bell and agbê | The colour of the terreiro |
| 8 | Caixa | 8 | Caixa | Drive |
| 9 | Pisada | 8 | Foot stamps, if confirmed | The beaten floor |
| 10 | Phrases | 16 | Alfaia phrases stack | Construction toward the rufo |
| 11 | Vertigem | 16 | Density rises | The vertiginous feel |
| 12 | Rufo | 4 | Final roll | The signature ending |
| 13 | Finale | 8 | Tutti and a cut | The close |

### e. Sound sources

- The alfaia, ganzá, agbê and agogô in the repository are reusable.
- Palmas and tambourine: VCSL, CC0.
- Hard or impossible to find:
  - **engomes**;
  - a true **second-head alfaia**, which would need re-pitching or EQ of existing samples;
  - **tabica**, still not identified;
  - **pisada** (foot stamps), which would have to be recorded or synthesized.
