# Research: brega, bregafunk and forró pé-de-serra

Research notes on three rhythms for the instrumental Batuque shows: names, instruments, patterns, tempo, show outlines and samples. Date: 2026-11-17.

No recording was analysed for these notes. Tempos and arrangement details marked [inferred] should
be measured or transcribed from recordings. Drum programming marked [inferred] is common producer
practice, not a cited source. No vocals or lyrics appear in the outlines.

## 1. Brega (bregapop)

### a. Name

- **In Recife, people just say «brega».** For this strand the local and press term is **«brega
  romântico»** (Revista Continente; UFPE undergraduate thesis by T. P. da Silva). The singer Loma
  says: «Em Recife, é só brega» (Correio Braziliense 2019, quoted in the UFPE master's dissertation
  by Rodolfo R. da Silva, 2023).
- **Academic term: «Bregapop».** Fontanella (UFPE, 2005), cited by R. R. da Silva (2023, pp. 28
  to 29), uses it for the urban brega of the North and Northeast. The same dissertation names three
  sub-strands: tecnobrega, bregapop and **brega-calypso**.
- Thiago Soares (UFPE; book _Ninguém é perfeito e a vida é assim_, 2017) describes three «eixos
  estéticos»:
  1. Reginaldo Rossi.
  2. Women singers, opened by the hit «Amor de Rapariga» (2002) and by Banda Calypso (from Pará)
     moving to Recife.
  3. «Funkização», which became bregafunk.
- **«Bregadeira»:** no source defines it, so the site does not use it.
- Recife and Pará name their grooves differently. The dominant Recife groove is nicknamed
  **«xacumbum»**. The Pará groove is the faster «calipso» (O Liberal 2021). Pará broadcasters
  called theirs «Cha Du Dum» (Jorge Reis, quoted in Chada, ANPPOM 2014).

### b. Instruments and roles

- **Teclado (keyboard):** «O instrumento principal é o teclado» (UFPE thesis; Diario de Pernambuco
  2020). It plays the hook in the intro and interlude, pads or strings, and stabs on a brass patch.
- **Guitarra (electric guitar):** the melodic lead. The typical sound is clean, with chorus and
  delay, playing riffs in thirds or sixths [inferred].
- **Baixo (bass)** and **bateria** (drums, often electronic): the base is «baixo, guitarra e
  bateria» (O Liberal; Revista Continente). In the Pará lineage, keyboards largely replaced the
  acoustic kit (Chada, ANPPOM 2013 and 2014).
- **Optional:** sax or brass, and percussion, mainly in Pará brega pop (Wikipedia, «Brega pop»).

### c. Named patterns, meter and tempo

- **Named grooves:** «xacumbum» (Recife) and «calipso» or «Cha Du Dum» (Pará). There is no
  catalogue of named beats like the maracatu baques.
- **Meter:** 4/4.
- **Tempo:** no source gives a figure. The Recife groove is described as slower than the Pará one.
  Expect about 130 to 150 BPM on a quarter-note pulse [inferred]. Measure it on recordings.
- **Form**, from the closely related Pará tecnobrega (Chada 2014): base beat, instrumental intro,
  A, an interlude with «vinhetas», chorus, B, instrumental coda. Tom fills on the drum machine
  («viradas») join the sections. The harmony has about four chords (I, IV, V).

### What makes it special

How a producer would program it [inferred; check on recordings]:

1. **The xacumbum groove:**
   - Kick on 1 and 3, with a pickup kick on step 8 or 16.
   - Snare or clap on 2 and 4.
   - Closed hat on the off-beat eighths, louder than the on-beats.
   - On the 16-step grid: kick 1, 9 (+16); snare 5, 13; hat 3, 7, 11, 15.
   - The light, bouncy «xa-cum-bum» feel comes from the off-beat hats and the pickup kick.
   - Swing: none or slight, about 0 to 10 %.
2. **Syncopated bass:** the root on 1, a push into the next chord on step 15 or 16, and octave
   jumps. It locks to the kick.
3. **The melody moves into an instrument:** the vocal hook is heard only on keyboard (a sax, brass
   or synth-lead patch) or on lead guitar in thirds. This is the core of the identity of the style.
4. **Keyboard texture:** a sustained string pad, plus off-beat chord stabs («teclado de brega»).
5. **A tom fill on the drum machine** at every section change, plus a one-bar stop before the
   chorus.

### d. Show outline

About 2:00 at about 140 BPM. One bar is about 1.7 s. 64 to 72 bars.

| #   | Scene                 | Bars   | What enters or changes                             | What it teaches                  |
| --- | --------------------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1   | Keyboard hook (intro) | 4      | Keyboard plays the melodic hook alone              | Brega is a keyboard music        |
| 2   | Xacumbum              | 4      | Kick, snare and off-beat hat                       | The base beat                    |
| 3   | Bass                  | 4      | Bass locks to the kick, pushes on step 16          | Syncopated bass                  |
| 4   | Harmony               | 4      | String pad and off-beat stabs, I, IV, V            | Simple harmony, keyboard texture |
| 5   | Guitar lead           | 8      | Chorus guitar in thirds plays verse A              | Romantic melody on guitar        |
| 6   | Virada                | 1      | Drum-machine tom fill                              | How sections join                |
| 7   | Refrão                | 8      | Keyboard lead plays the chorus melody, full band   | The hook                         |
| 8   | Stop and return       | 2      | One bar of silence or kick only, then a hit        | The break before the chorus      |
| 9   | Brega and calypso     | 8      | Same loop, hats doubled to sixteenths, faster feel | Recife and Pará                  |
| 10  | Interlude «vinheta»   | 4      | Brass-patch stabs answer the guitar                | The interlude device             |
| 11  | Final chorus          | 8      | Guitar and keyboard in harmony                     | Everything together              |
| 12  | Coda                  | 2 to 4 | Hook, final tom fill, stop on 1                    | Ending                           |

### e. Samples

- **Easy:** CC0 drum-machine kicks, snares and hats are plentiful on Freesound.
- **Harder:** CC0 string pads and brass stabs exist only in scattered form. A chorus electric guitar
  needs a multi-sample or a synthesized pluck. The keyboard leads are easy to synthesize.

## 2. Bregafunk

### a. Name

- **Names:**
  - In Recife: just «brega».
  - National and academic term: **«bregafunk»**, also «bregafunk».
  - Other names found: tecno brega, arrocha-brega, eletro brega and «brega de passinho» (Correio
    Braziliense 2019; Revista Continente).
- **Academic sources (UFPE):**
  - Franckel Moreau, master's dissertation in anthropology (2022).
  - R. R. da Silva, master's dissertation in communication (2023). It calls bregafunk a hybrid of
    funk and tecnobrega, citing Gomes (UFPE doctoral thesis, 2013).
  - Ariel S. N. da Silva, undergraduate thesis (2021).
- **Dates:** Billboard Brasil says it has existed since 2009 and became national from 2018
  («Envolvimento», MC Loma). Wikipedia PT says around 2011.

### b. Sound sources and producer terms

- **Drum and bass terms used by Recife producers:**
  - **Bumbo**, the kick.
  - **Caixa**, especially the metallic **«lata»** snare, «uma das assinaturas do bregafunk desde
    2018» (Diario de Pernambuco 2025, on the producer Gordinho Bolado).
  - **Chimbais dobrados**, doubled hats.
  - **«Graves»**, sub or 808 bass.
  - All of these appear in the account of the producer Dany Bala in VICE.
- The style moved away from the **«tamborzão carioca»** (VICE).
- The minimal beat is «baixo, kick e uma caixa associada a uma lata» (Diario de Pernambuco 2025).
- **Melodic sources:** MIDI piano chords, synths, filtered guitars, MIDI horns that recall frevo
  brass, and Casio-like patches (Mixmag; Wikipedia).
- **Workflow:** made in FL Studio (Mixmag).
- **Lead hook:** a short melodic hook on a synth or wind patch carries many tracks [inferred].
- **Vocal chops:** not essential to the style, so the show leaves them out.

### c. Tempo, with history

From VICE (Duca do Baixo, MC Tocha, Dany Bala):

- Funk was at 130 BPM.
- A track of that time by one of these producers was at 150 BPM.
- Bregafunk climbed to 190 to 195 BPM around 2011 to 2014.
- Duca slowed it to about 170. Then a later hit set **about 160, up to 170, still common today**.
- Commercial sample packs notate it in half time (87 BPM).
- The «160 to 180 BPM, half-time feel» on Wikipedia is consistent with this.

**Recommendation:** run the show at **about 170 BPM, felt at 170**. One turn of the 16-step wheel
is one bar at 170 (about 1.41 s): the chimbal in sixteenths of 170, the bumbo on beats 1 and 3, the
lata in 3+3+2 per half bar. The half-time count of the sample packs is only how they write it; the
show is built at the felt tempo (D10 in `docs/decisions.md`).

There are no canonical named patterns. There are trend names and the dance, the «passinho dos
malokas».

### What makes it special

How a producer would program it [inferred], on a 16-step bar at 170:

1. **A dembow-like kick and lata:**
   - Kick on 1 and 9, plus a ghost note on 7 or 15.
   - Lata on 4, 7, 12 and 15, the 3+3+2 feel. Dany Bala calls the style «closer to reggaeton than
     funk».
   - The lata is loud and bright, the kick full.
   - The space on steps 2 to 3 and 10 to 11 makes it bounce.
   - No swing. Everything is hard-quantized (FL Studio).
2. **Doubled hats:** sixteenths or 32nd-note rolls at low velocity (about 60 to 70 %), accents on
   the off-beats, rolls before a drop.
3. **Sliding 808 «graves»:** a long sub that glides between roots, sidechained to the kick.
4. **The lead hook:** a short call of 1 or 2 bars, in a minor key, repeated over and over.
5. **Drop and breaks:**
   - Strip to the hook plus hats.
   - One beat of silence.
   - The drop, with everything at once.
   - Optional: a MIDI frevo-brass stab as the «Recife signature».

### d. Show outline

About 2:00 at 170 BPM. This first plan counted half-time bars of about 2.8 s, about 42 bars; at
the felt 170 a bar is about 1.41 s, so the show doubles each scene's bars (D10).

| #   | Scene              | Bars | What enters or changes                       | What it teaches         |
| --- | ------------------ | ---- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| 1   | Hook alone         | 2    | Lead hook, no beat                           | The hook                |
| 2   | Bumbo              | 2    | Kick on 1 and 9                              | The pulse on 1 and 3    |
| 3   | The lata           | 4    | Metallic snare, 3+3+2                        | The signature sound     |
| 4   | Chimbais dobrados  | 2    | Sixteenth hats, then rolls                   | Motion                  |
| 5   | Graves             | 4    | Sliding 808                                  | The bass                |
| 6   | Full beat          | 4    | Hook and chords (MIDI piano)                 | The whole groove        |
| 7   | Old and new        | 4    | Same beat at 190, then back to 170           | Tempo history (VICE)    |
| 8   | Tamborzão contrast | 2    | Carioca tamborzão for 2 bars, then bregafunk | Why it is not Rio funk  |
| 9   | Break              | 2    | Strip to hook and hats, then a silent beat   | The build               |
| 10  | Drop               | 4    | Everything, plus a frevo-brass stab          | The drop                |
| 11  | Passinho variation | 4    | Kick doubled, lata syncopation varied        | Variation for the dance |
| 12  | Finale             | 4    | Hook, final lata, cut                        | Ending                  |

Scene 7 changes the tempo inside the show. The decision since then is one tempo per show, so the
sketch leaves it out.

### e. Samples

- **Easy:** CC0 kicks, snares, 808s and hats are plentiful on Freesound. A tin-can hit works as the
  «lata»: Freesound has 115 CC0 results for «tin can hit».
- **Lead hook:** synthesize it.
- **Avoid:** commercial bregafunk kits (Kit Maloka, RVerso). They are licensed royalty-free, not
  CC0.

## 3. Forró pé-de-serra

### a. Name

- **Name:** «forró pé-de-serra», also «forró tradicional», means the trio of sanfona, zabumba and
  triângulo, from the 1940s (UFAL dissertation; Wikipedia).
- **Contested labels:** pé-de-serra, eletrônico and universitário are disputed terms (Thiago
  Paulino, UFS doctoral thesis, 2017). Forró universitário reworks pé-de-serra with borrowed
  elements (Unisinos article).
- Forró universitário bands keep the pé-de-serra base and add elements of choro, samba, salsa and
  reggae. They also add instruments: rabeca, bandolim, cavaquinho and zabumbatera.
- **For the show:** it teaches pé-de-serra with the trio, plus the extra instruments of forró
  universitário bands.

### b. Instruments and roles

- **Sanfona (accordion):** melody, intros and interludes, octave doubling, and repeated notes on
  the bellows, the «jogo de fole» (Côrtes, Per Musi 2014).
- **Zabumba:**
  - The low head (maceta or pele) marks the pulse with low accents.
  - The **bacalhau** stick on the high bottom head plays the off-beats.
  - Côrtes and Wikipedia («Zabumba») describe both.
- **Triângulo:** steady sixteenths with an **open accent on the off-beat**, made when the left hand
  opens (Côrtes; Wikipedia).
- **Forró universitário extras:** rabeca and bandolim (melody doubling), cavaquinho and violão
  (chords), bass (contrabaixo), zabumbatera (zabumba plus drum kit) and pandeiro.

### c. Named patterns, meter and tempo

- **Named patterns:** baião, xote, xaxado, arrasta-pé, and «forró» as a levada. They play the role
  the baques play in maracatu.
- **Baião feel:** a zabumba figure of a dotted eighth and a sixteenth, tied over (Côrtes). The
  accordion uses 3+3+2 accents that fit the bacalhau. Melodies are in mixolydian or dorian.
- **Meter:** 2/4, traditionally. The show uses an 8-step wheel, one bar per turn.
- **Tempo:** the sources disagree. Only the order is solid: xote is slower than baião, and baião is
  slower than arrasta-pé.
  - Thiago Brisolla (USP dissertation, via a search snippet; the server was down): xote 70 to 80.
  - Web sources (Melodigging): xote 85 to 110, baião 110 to 135, arrasta-pé 130 to 160.
  - The sketch uses 120 BPM [inferred]. Measure it on recordings.

### What makes it special

How a producer would program it:

1. **The zabumba conversation:**
   - A low-head accent on beat 1 and on the «a» of beat 2: the tied figure of a dotted eighth and a
     sixteenth (Côrtes).
   - Bacalhau clicks on the off-beat eighths.
   - Velocity: low head 100 %, bacalhau 50 to 70 %.
2. **Triângulo:**
   - All 16 steps closed, at about 60 %.
   - An open accent on every off-beat eighth, at 100 % (Côrtes).
   - A slight lilt, about 8 to 12 % swing, which makes it «dance».
3. **Sanfona melody:**
   - Mixolydian or dorian scale.
   - Starts on an anacrusis, arpeggios up to the minor seventh, thirds and sixths (Côrtes).
   - It also carries the vocal tune.
4. **3+3+2 bellows accents** against the 2/4 pulse.
5. **Stops («breques»)** with a zabumba roll back in [inferred].

### d. Show outline

About 2:00 at about 120 BPM. This first plan counted four-four wheels of about 2 s, about 60 wheels.

| #   | Scene                         | Wheels | What enters or changes                | What it teaches                   |
| --- | ----------------------------- | ------ | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 1   | Triângulo                     | 4      | Closed sixteenths, open off-beat      | The pulse and the open accent     |
| 2   | Zabumba pele                  | 4      | Low-head figure                       | The bass marking                  |
| 3   | Bacalhau                      | 4      | Off-beat clicks answer the low head   | The conversation of the two heads |
| 4   | The trio                      | 2      | Sanfona chord rhythm                  | Pé-de-serra is three players      |
| 5   | Sanfona intro                 | 8      | Intro melody, octave doubling         | The accordion leads               |
| 6   | Baixo and violão              | 4      | Bass, cavaquinho                      | The universitário band            |
| 7   | Melody on rabeca and bandolim | 8      | Tune doubled                          | The universitário colours         |
| 8   | Jogo de fole                  | 4      | Bellows 3+3+2 accents                 | The bellows trick                 |
| 9   | Xote                          | 6      | Tempo down to about 90, xote levada   | Named pattern 1                   |
| 10  | Baião                         | 6      | About 120, baião figure               | Named pattern 2                   |
| 11  | Arrasta-pé                    | 6      | About 150, fast two-step levada       | Named pattern 3                   |
| 12  | Breque                        | 1      | Stop, zabumba roll                    | The break                         |
| 13  | Finale                        | 4      | All at baião tempo, final accent on 1 | Everything                        |

Scenes 9 and 11 change the tempo inside the show. The decision since then is one tempo per show, so
the sketch keeps only the baião.

### e. Samples

- **Triângulo:** Freesound has 192 CC0 sounds, open and closed.
- **Accordion:** Freesound has 78 CC0 «accordion note» sounds. A full chromatic multi-sample is
  uncertain, so synthesis (a detuned reed) may be needed.
- **Hardest:** Freesound has **no «zabumba» sounds**. Use a CC0 bass-drum or surdo hit for the low
  head and a stick click for the bacalhau, or record one.
- **Rabeca:** no CC0 source was checked. It is likely hard to find.
