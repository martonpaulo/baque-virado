## How to read this

- **[S]**: stated in the cited source, which was read in full or in the quoted part.
- **[S2]**: secondhand. The source cites another author who was not read.
- **[I]**: inference from musical practice. It is not a citation. Check it by ear before building on
  it.
- **Step grids**: 16 steps are 16 sixteenth notes, so two bars of 2/4 or one bar of 4/4. `X` is an
  accent, `x` a normal hit, `o` an open or high tone, `.` a rest.
- Numbers in brackets, such as [3], point to the [References](#references).

## 0. A shared frame: tresillo and contrametricity

- Sandroni names two rhythmic paradigms. The **paradigma do tresillo** is the 19th-century figure
  of lundu, habanera, tango and maxixe. The **paradigma do Estácio** is a 16-pulse cycle that 1930s
  Rio samba consolidated. He also discusses _contrametricidade_: placing accents against the strong
  beat [S2, 1].
- Accenting the first onset of each group gives the tresillo its 3+3+2 grouping: `X..X..X.` [S2].
- [I] **A working hypothesis for the whole show.** Most of these rhythms stack a steady pulse
  (gonguê, ganzá, triangle, hi-hat) against a 3+3+2 or contrametric bass figure. The pull between
  where the body expects the beat and where the drum lands is a plausible source of drive. What
  changes from rhythm to rhythm is the meaning: sacred, militant, carnival, nostalgic or erotic.

## 1. Maracatu de baque virado

**Rhythm.**

- The ensemble is percussion and voice only: gonguê (one bell), tarol or caixa de guerra, at least
  twelve alfaias, and mineiro (ganzá) [S2, 2].
- «Baque virado» means «baque dobrado», the doubled beat of several bass drums [S2, 2, after
Guerra-Peixe 1952].
- The gonguê is the highest instrument. It sets the tempo and plays the cell that «structures and
  directs» the group. The cell changes from nation to nation; the function stays [S, 2].
- The caixas play continuous sixteenths. Their accents create the _balanço_ [S, 2].
- The alfaias are the «heart of the batuque» and carry the main «rhythmic melody». They split into
  low, middle and high voices: Melê, Biancó and Yan in Nação Porto Rico; Marcante, Repique and
  Meião in Leão Coroado. The low drums keep the marcação. Its constant figure across nations is
  **sixteenth, eighth, sixteenth** [S, 2].
- The agbê cell is eighth, sixteenth, sixteenth. The ganzá is taught as «TUmaraCATUmaraCATU»: four
  sixteenths, accents on the 1st and 4th [S, 2].
- _Viradas_ are breaks that interrupt the repetition or bring in a new baque [S, 2].
- Tempo: no source gives a BPM. [I] Nações walk at a heavy processional pace, roughly 80 to 110 BPM.
  Measure recordings before fixing a number.

**Melody and harmony.** The _toadas_ are sung by a lead singer and answered by the chorus. [I] The
melodies are short, stepwise and modal-major, with no harmony instrument.

**Social context and feeling.**

- «Nação» means a group tied to candomblé or xangô, with strong community bonds and a strong sense
  of tradition [S2, 2].
- The iron bell is linked to Ogum, the orixá who works iron [S, 2].
- [I] **The feeling is weight and dignity: a royal court on the move.** The mass of alfaias is felt
  in the chest. Unison breaks after a virada make a crowd breathe together.

**2-second signature.** Many low drums on the marcação, a high bell, a hissing ganzá.

## 2. Coco da Xambá

**Rhythm.**

- In coco de roda, a _tirador_ (solo singer) gives the line and the circle answers. The _umbigada_
  invites the next dancer into the ring. Variants include coco de roda, de umbigada, de embolada and
  de zambê [S, 3; search summary].
- In _coco de trupé_ (Arcoverde), dancers stamp wooden _tamancos_ on the floor, with triângulo,
  pandeiro, surdo and ganzá [S, 4; search summary].
- **What sets Xambá apart.** The alfaia is louder and faster. It sits on a chair so both heads can
  be struck. The coco is built from «rhythmic phrases that join together and end in a different
  _rufo_» [S2, 6, after Alves 2006].
- The ritual drums are the _engomes_: played by hand, always in a trio (melé, melé-ancó, ian), found
  only in Xambá. Gonguê and agbê play with them [S, 6].
- Xambá coco is faster than other coco traditions [5; search summary, unverified].
- [I] Tempo is roughly 110 to 140 BPM. The ganzá runs continuous sixteenths. The low alfaia marks
  beats 1 and 2 with off-beat pick-ups.

**Melody.** [I] Short verses with fixed refrains in call and response, mostly major, in a narrow
range a chorus of non-singers can sing. The repertoire is _loas_ and _toadas_, some traditional,
some by members of the community [S, 6].

**Social context and feeling.**

- The Xambá terreiro (Ilê Axé Oyá Meguê, São Benedito, Olinda) has Bantu origins. Mãe Biu led it. In
  2006 it became an urban quilombo, the first in the Northeast [S, 6].
- Coco da Xambá was first played only at the festivals of the terreiro. It has existed for «more
  than 50 years» [S, 6].
- Groups of the Xambá community took it to the stage in the early 2000s, around the coco feast of
  **29 June** [S, 6, 7].
- In candomblé, each deity has its own songs and drum toques. They carry the «state of mind,
  sensations and emotions» the deity awakens [S2, 6, after Segato 1999].
- [I] **The feeling is a family party in the yard: joy, belonging, Black pride.** It is profane, but
  it comes from a sacred house.

**2-second signature.** Ganzá and palmas over a thundering two-headed alfaia, with a call answered
at once by a chorus.

## 3. Frevo

**Rhythm.**

- Frevo is «frenetic carnival dance music». It emerged around 1900 in Recife, among carnival clubs
  of Black and mixed-race urban workers. It grew out of «the marches and polkas» of military-style
  bands, and its dance steps «developed largely from the Capoeira». Crook distinguishes _frevo de
  rua_ (instrumental), _frevo de bloco_ (strings, percussion, female chorus) and _frevo canção_
  [S, 8].
- Meter is 2/4. A common tempo is about 120 BPM, reaching about 180 in performance [S, 9; search
summary].
- All Pernambuco frevos share one percussion cell. Caixa and surdo play **twelve sixteenths with
  specific accents, then a _rullo_**, filling two bars of 2/4 [S, 10].
- The introduction «already is the music itself». It «invariably starts with an anacrusis»,
  bursting in headlong [S2, 10, after Oliveira 1971].
- A standard orchestra has 2 alto, 2 tenor and 1 baritone saxophone (_palhetas_), plus 4 trumpets
  and 4 trombones (_metais_) [S, 10].
- Sub-styles: _frevo-coqueiro_ (high trumpet notes), _frevo-ventania_ (sax sixteenth runs) and
  _abafo_ (two bands play loud to drown each other out). These come from teaching sources, weaker
  than the rest [S, 11].

**Melody and harmony.** [I] Brass and reeds answer each other. Trumpets and trombones throw short
chords; the saxes reply with chromatic sixteenth runs. Phrases are 4 or 8 bars, in major keys with
secondary dominants, inherited from band marches.

**Social context and feeling.**

- The music and the _passo_ were born together. Capoeiras walking ahead of the military bands gave
  the dance its moves [S2, 12, after Oliveira].
- [I] **The feeling is euphoric, controlled chaos with a fighting edge.** It is the street fully open
  at carnival. The abafo turns the music into a duel.

**2-second signature.** A headlong pick-up into a brass blast, then running caixa sixteenths with a
roll every second bar.

## 4. Manguebeat

**Rhythm and sound.**

- The sound of Chico Science & Nação Zumbi is «a thundering drum core, booming bass, driving
  keyboard, and distorted electric guitar», with a heavy backbeat «vaguely reminiscent of a bluesy,
  clave-based Bo Diddley beat». The guitar «pounds out… fuzzy, aggressive-sounding chords» [S, 13].
- The movement revived maracatu, ciranda, embolada, coco, repente and pastoril. It mixed them with
  hip-hop, soul, funk, rock, reggae, dub, samba-reggae, punk and post-punk [S, 13].
- Galinsky reads «A cidade» as «a reinterpretation of the ciranda rhythm combined with a rock-funk
  feel» [S2, 13].
- Herom Vargas reads the band through «hybridism» (_Hibridismos musicais de Chico Science & Nação
  Zumbi_, 2007) [S, 13, 14].
- [I] Tempo is about 90 to 105 BPM. The alfaias play the maracatu marcação. A rock kit plays the
  backbeat. Guitar and bass lock to the alfaia accents, not the snare.

**Melody and harmony.** [I] Riffs, not songs: one or two chords, power chords or pentatonic and
dorian riffs, with a half-spoken vocal.

**Social context and feeling.**

- The manifesto «Caranguejos com cérebro» is by Fred Zero Quatro. It was printed in the notes of _Da
  lama ao caos_ (1994), when Recife was called the «fourth worst city in the world». Its central
  image is an **«antenna stuck in the mud»**: local roots connected to the global [S, 13].
- Chico Science: «Só vale com a diversão levada a sério» (it only counts if fun is taken seriously)
  [S2, 13].
- In «Manguetown», day is work and exploitation, and night is community and dreaming. Sneed hears
  «an undeniable sense of hope» in the ambivalence [S, 13].
- [I] **The feeling is angry hope**: mud, heat, the city and the crab, with a groove that refuses
  pity.

**2-second signature.** Alfaias and a distorted guitar on the same accents, over a rock backbeat.

## 5. Forró pé-de-serra

**Rhythm.**

- The baião has «a syncopated duple rhythm played on the zabumba and a melodic scale featuring a
  flatted seventh». In the 1940s Luiz Gonzaga built the urban baião from a viola rhythm of
  northeastern bards and the drum patterns of _bandas de pífanos_. The usual line-up is accordion,
  triangle and zabumba [S, 15].
- The «standard» baião cell is a reduction. Gonzaga's recordings show many accompaniment patterns
  [S, 16; abstract only].
- The triangle keeps sixteenths and opens on the off-beats. The zabumba gives the bass pulse [S, 17;
search summary].
- [I] Tempo: baião about 85 to 110 BPM, xote slower, arrasta-pé faster.

**Melody and harmony.**

- A major scale with a minor seventh (mixolydian) is one baião variant [S2, after Guerra-Peixe].
  Melodies are often modal, mixolydian or dorian [S, 18; a teaching source].
- [I] Lydian dominant (raised 4th, flat 7th) also appears in sanfona lines. Short motifs repeat over
  a drone-like I and bVII.

**Social context and feeling.** Trotta makes four points [S, 19]:

- In Gonzaga's work, migration sets city life against the sertão. This makes **saudade
  «transversal» to almost his whole repertoire**. The Northeast becomes a «space of saudade».
- The sertão becomes «an idealized mirage of whoever is far away».
- The sanfona timbre is «analogous to the organ and the human choir», sounds with great «power of
  evocation» in Catholic liturgy.
- The fast pumping of the bellows, the _fungadinho_, invites dancing and flirtation.

The trio of sanfona, zabumba and triângulo, led by the sanfona, gives the «characteristic sound» of
a shared Northeastern imaginary [S, 19].

[I] **The feeling is bittersweet: dancing close while missing home.** The flat seventh leans without
resolving, which may be why it reads as longing.

**2-second signature.** The open and closed triangle, the low «tum… tum-tum» of the zabumba, and a
sanfona phrase landing on the flat seventh.

## 6. Brega

**Sound.**

- The canonical marks of romantic brega are «the vast use of keyboards», drum _viradas_ «as
  foundations for the build-up of the choruses», and singing «with wide use of range and vibrato».
  Later arrangements add guitars and electronic drums [S, 20].
- [I] Tempo is a mid-tempo two-step or slow bolero feel, about 70 to 100 BPM. Harmony uses major
  ballad progressions (I, vi, IV, V), with a key change up a step for the last chorus. Sax or
  keyboard answers each vocal line. No musicological source was found for this.

**Social context and feeling.**

- Brega takes the «melancholic» tone of Latin American melodrama. Its songs are spaces «of love,
  saudade and sexuality (_sacanagem_)». For working-class listeners, the romantic ideal is a small
  way to transgress class domination [S, 20].
- Thiago Soares's book _Ninguém é perfeito e a vida é assim_ (2017) maps the scene «from Reginaldo
  Rossi to MC Troinha» in three aesthetic axes, starting with Rossi's 1966 debut. Soares reads brega
  as melodrama [S, 21].
- Until 2017, brega could not play at the official Carnival or apply for public funding. Recife law
  01/2021 declared it intangible heritage [S, 22].
- The figure of the «suffering cuckold who drinks» in Rossi, Waldick Soriano and Bartô Galeno
  appears in an Intercom paper [S2, 23; search summary].
- [I] **The feeling is public heartbreak, enjoyed together**: sofrência sung loud at the bar table.
  Exaggeration is the point.

**2-second signature.** A bright keyboard pad, a drum fill and a big vibrato line, carried by sax or
synth in the instrumental form.

## 7. Bregafunk

**Sound.**

- The style formed around 2008 to 2009, when Rio funk merged with local brega in the peripheries of
  Recife. Thiago Soares dates it «since 2009», with national attention in 2018. MC Loma's
  «Envolvimento» sounds like funk but is not: «reggae keyboards», synthesized brass inspired by
  Carnival, and «a sharp hit that sounds like a click» [S, 22].
- Mixmag describes a «clinking, offbeat rhythm» like the forró triangle, «complex ever-changing drum
  patterns», sliding bass, piano chords that shift key like reggae, MIDI horns that mimic frevo
  brass and cheap Casio-like patches, made in FL Studio. The music is «both sexy and aggressive»
  [S, 24].
- **Tamborzão, the Rio ancestor.** DJ Luciano built it in the atabaque line: «tum-papá-pum-pá». DJ
  Marcelo André calls it «a three-second loop… without a fill». The earlier Volt Mix had sixteenth
  hats, snare on the second halves of beats, and a kick «syncopating three of the sixteen
  divisions» [S, 25].
- Tempo: 160 to 180 BPM with a half-time feel [S, 26; secondary]. The show is built felt at 170:
  one 16-step bar at 170, the bumbo on beats 1 and 3 (D26 in `docs/decisions.md`).

**Social context and feeling.**

- The _passinho dos malokas_ dance drew «violent looks and actions», and the genre was branded
  «vulgar and sexualized». The Aluno Conectado tablet programme (2012 to 2014) sparked interest in
  music software among young people [S, 22].
- Ethnographies by Moreau (2022) and Paz, both at UFPE [S, 27, 28; abstracts only].
- [I] **The feeling is swagger and play**: a bodily challenge in the passinho, with romantic brega
  melody under the provocation.

**2-second signature.** A bounce of dry kick (on 1 and 3 at 170) and click, a clinking off-beat
like a triangle, sliding bass, and a cheap bright synth hook.

## Design notes

Every grid is **[I]**: a starting point for composition, not a transcription. Check each on
recordings.

### Maracatu de baque virado

```text
step    1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
gongue  X . x . x . X . . x . x X . x .   [I]
alfaia  X x . x X . . . X x . x X . x .   marcação 16-8-16 [S] + variation [I]
caixa   X x x X x x X x x X x x X x X x   continuous 16ths, accents [I]
ganza   X x x X X x x X X x x X X x x X   accents 1 and 4 of each beat [S]
```

- **Feeling.** A royal procession: weighty, sacred, collective.
- **Melody must have.** A short toada phrase, stepwise, that a crowd could answer. Long held notes
  over the drums. One unison break after each virada as the climax.
- **Avoid.** Ending lines on the downbeat in unison with the alfaias. Generic «tribal» toms. Harmony
  pads. Speeding up into a batucada.

### Coco da Xambá

```text
step    1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
alfaia  X . . x X . x . X . . x X . x .   [I] both heads
ganza   X x x x X x x x X x x x X x x x   [I]
palmas  . . X . . . X . . . X . . . X .   [I] offbeats
trupe   X . . . X . . . X . . . X . . .   [I] feet
```

- **Feeling.** A yard party at a sacred house: joyful, rooted, proud.
- **Melody must have.** A 2-bar call and a 2-bar answer, the same every round, inside an octave. A
  closing _rufo_ on the alfaia ends each section [S].
- **Avoid.** Turning it into forró, with sanfona or triangle in the lead. Slowing it into a lament.
  Orixá imagery as decoration: the Xambá toques are religious property, so take the rhythmic feel,
  not ritual songs.

### Frevo de rua

```text
step    1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
caixa   X x x X x x X x x X x X r r r r   12 16ths + rullo [S]; accent places [I]
surdo   X . . . X . . X X . . . X . . .   [I]
metais  . . X . . X . . X . . . . . X .   stabs [I]
```

- **Feeling.** Euphoric, headlong, combative.
- **Melody must have.** An anacrusis that bursts in [S]. Trumpet stabs answered by sax runs.
  Chromatic approach notes. One abafo moment where two lines compete in volume.
- **Avoid.** The sweetness of frevo canção. Stiff quantized sixteenths. A downbeat start with no
  pick-up. The overused «Vassourinhas» quote.

### Manguebeat

```text
step    1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
alfaia  X x . x X . . x . x . x X . x .   [I]
snare   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .   backbeat [I]
guitar  X . . X . . X . . . X . X . . .   riff locked to alfaia [I]
```

- **Feeling.** Angry hope: mud, heat, the crab with an antenna.
- **Melody must have.** A distorted riff of one or two chords on the alfaia accents. A rhythmic,
  speech-like lead line, embolada-like. One local folk quote, a ciranda or coco turn, as the
  «antenna».
- **Avoid.** Rock with drums pasted on. Clean guitar. Copying the riffs of «Maracatu atômico» or «A
  praieira». Shiny production.

### Forró pé-de-serra

```text
step     1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
zabumba  X . . x X . . . X . . x X . . .   low: dotted 8th + 16th + 8th [I, conventional reduction]
bacalhau . . o . . . o . . . o . . . o .   high stick [I]
triangle x x o x x x o x x x o x x x o x   closed/open, offbeats open [S]
```

- **Feeling.** Bittersweet closeness: dancing tight while missing home.
- **Melody must have.** Mixolydian, with the flat seventh on a strong beat [S]. Repeated 2-bar
  motifs. Bellows accents, the fungadinho [S]. A pick-up into a held note.
- **Avoid.** Quoting «Asa Branca». One «standard» cell for the whole show: vary it as Gonzaga did
  [S]. Electronic forró gloss. Sad minor keys: saudade here sits in major with a flat seventh.

### Brega

```text
step    1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
kick    X . . . . . X . X . . . . . . .   [I]
snare   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .   [I]
keys    X . X . X . X . X . X . X . X .   pulsing chords [I]
fill    . . . . . . . . . . . . X x x x   virada into chorus [S]
```

- **Feeling.** Heartbreak enjoyed together: exaggerated, warm, shameless.
- **Melody must have.** A singable, wide line with long notes for vibrato [S]. A drum fill before
  every chorus [S]. A key change up a step on the last chorus [I]. Sax or synth answering each
  phrase.
- **Avoid.** Irony: Soares reads the genre as melodrama to take seriously. Bregafunk drums. A sad
  minor key throughout, since brega cries in major. The show decision still sets a minor key for
  saudade, so test both.

### Bregafunk

```text
step    1 . . . 5 . . . 9 . . . 13. . .
kick    X . . X . . X . X . . X . . . .   3+3+2 family [I]
click   . . X . . . X . . . X . . . X x   offbeat «clink» [S]; positions [I]
bass    X . . . . . X . X . . . . . . .   sliding [S]; positions [I]
```

- **Feeling.** Swagger and play: a body challenge with a sweet hook.
- **Melody must have.** A short, cheap and bright synth hook, repeated. Horns that mimic frevo [S]
  fit the series. A reggae-like keyboard off-beat [S]. Space for a dance break.
- **Avoid.** Plain tamborzão, which is Rio, not Recife. Copying a sample hook from a commercial hit.
  Sexual vocal chops: the show is instrumental.
