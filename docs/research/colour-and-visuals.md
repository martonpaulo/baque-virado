## 1. Colour and emotion

### Confirmed

- Valdez and Mehrabian (1994), "Effects of color on emotions", *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 123(4), 394–409, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/ . They used Munsell chips and the Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance model. Brightness and saturation moved emotion far more than hue did. The standardized regressions in the abstract are:
  - Pleasure = .69 Brightness + .22 Saturation.
  - Arousal = −.31 Brightness + .60 Saturation.
  - Dominance = −.76 Brightness + .32 Saturation.
  - In short, brightness raises pleasure, saturation raises arousal, and darkness plus saturation reads as dominant. (I took the coefficients from abstract snippets. The PubMed page would not load here.)
- Wilms and Oberfeld (2018), "Color and emotion: effects of hue, saturation, and brightness", *Psychological Research* 82, 896–914, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-017-0880-8 (PDF: https://www.staff.uni-mainz.de/oberfeld/downloads/Wilms-Oberfeld2018_Article_ColorAndEmotionEffectsOfHueSat.pdf ). I read the abstract in the PDF.
  - 27 colorimetrically controlled colours were shown on an LED display to 62 people.
  - Saturated, bright colours gave higher arousal and stronger skin-conductance responses.
  - Arousal rose with hue from blue and green to red.
  - Valence was highest for saturated, bright colours.
  - The dimensions interact. For example, blue was the most pleasant hue, but only at high saturation.
- Jonauskaite et al. (2020), "Universal Patterns in Color-Emotion Associations Are Further Shaped by Linguistic and Geographic Proximity", *Psychological Science* 31(10), 1245–1260, https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797620948810 .
  - 4,598 people in 30 nations matched 20 emotions to 12 colour terms.
  - Associations were largely universal (mean r = .88), with local variation tied to language and geography.
  - Brazil was not one of the 30 nations, as far as I could check. This is an uncertainty.

### Secondary

- Science Focus's summary of Jonauskaite et al. (https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/revealing-the-psychology-of-colour-to-be-a-rainbow-rollercoaster-of-emotion): red ↔ love and anger, yellow ↔ joy, black and grey ↔ sadness, white ↔ relief. I could not open the paper's figures to verify the pink and brown pairings.

### Inference

- Hue matters least. Feeling should mostly be carried by saturation and brightness, with hue as the cultural signal.
- Red is ambivalent (love and anger), so context and motion decide which one it reads as.

## 2. Music and colour crossmodal correspondences

### Confirmed

- Palmer, Schloss, Xu and Prado-León (2013), "Music–color associations are mediated by emotion", *PNAS* 110(22), 8836–8841, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3670360 .
  - The study tested US and Mexican listeners.
  - Fast, major-mode music was matched to lighter, more saturated, yellower colours.
  - Slow, minor-mode music was matched to darker, desaturated, bluer colours.
  - The emotion ratings of the music correlated with those of the chosen colours at .89 < r < .99, which the authors read as emotional mediation.
- Spence (2011), "Crossmodal correspondences: A tutorial review", *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics* 73, 971–995, https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-010-0073-7 .
  - People consistently match high-pitched sounds with small, bright objects placed high in space.
  - The review argues these correspondences help the brain bind sight and sound, alongside timing and space.
  - I read the abstract only, not the full section on lightness.
- Reymore and Lindsey (2025), "Color and tone color: audiovisual crossmodal correspondences with musical instrument timbre", *Frontiers in Psychology* 15, 1520131, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1520131 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11747214/).
  - Timbre ratings of "bright, high, light, happy" predicted lighter colours, and "dark, low, heavy, sad" predicted darker ones, even at fixed pitch.
  - The effects were weaker for saturation than for lightness.
  - Trumpet leaned to red, orange and yellow; clarinet leaned to blue. The authors flag this as instrument-specific.
- Adeli, Rouat and Molotchnikoff (2014), "Audiovisual correspondence between musical timbre and visual shapes", *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 8, 352, https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00352 . Harsh timbres matched jagged shapes and soft timbres matched smooth ones. Colour had no effect on this.

### Inference

- Bass-heavy, low material (alfaia, paredão sub-bass, manguebeat guitars) belongs with darker fields.
- Bright, high material (frevo brass, agogô, ganzá) belongs with light accents.
- Harsh timbres call for jagged geometry, and soft ones for round geometry. Shape carries timbre better than hue does.

## 3. Rhythm-driven animation and safety

### Confirmed

- **Motion beats flashes for beat sense.**
  - Iversen, Patel, Nicodemus and Emmorey (2015), "Synchronization to auditory and visual rhythms in hearing and deaf individuals", *Cognition* 134, 232–244, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4255154 . A silent bouncing ball with a clear impact point drove tapping nearly as well as sound.
  - Hove, Iversen, Zhang and Repp (2013), "Synchronization with competing visual and auditory rhythms: bouncing ball meets metronome", *Psychological Research* 77, 388–398. Hove et al. (2012) is at https://sccn.ucsd.edu/~jiversen/pdfs/hove_etal_2012.pdf . A moving visual rhythm can perturb timing as much as an auditory one.
  - This literature notes that discrete flashes give a weak sense of beat.
- **Motion carries the same emotion as music.** Sievers, Polansky, Casey and Wheatley (2013), "Music and movement share a dynamic structure that supports universal expressions of emotion", *PNAS* 110(1), 70–75, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3538264 .
  - People set five features to express an emotion: rate, jitter (irregularity), direction, step size, and dissonance or visual spikiness.
  - Each emotion got the same settings in music and in an animated ball's motion.
  - The result held in rural Cambodia. I did not retrieve the per-emotion values.
- **Audiovisual synchrony window.**
  - van Wassenhove, Grant and Poeppel (2007), "Temporal window of integration in auditory-visual speech perception", *Neuropsychologia* 45, 598–607. For speech they found an asymmetric window of about 200 ms, robust from about −30 ms to +170 ms of audio lag.
  - Cecere, Gross and Thut (2016), "Behavioural evidence for separate mechanisms of audiovisual temporal binding as a function of leading sensory modality", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4915493/ . The window is narrow when sound leads and wider when vision leads.
  - Stevenson and Wallace (2014) define the binding window as the range of onset asynchronies that still alter responses. I saw this only through citing papers.
- **Looming captures attention.** Franconeri and Simons (2003), "Moving and looming stimuli capture attention", *Perception & Psychophysics* 65(7), 999–1010, https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/FranconeriSimons_Motion.pdf .
  - Expanding objects captured attention and receding ones did not.
  - Later work contests this: motion onset alone can capture. See the review in https://www.scielo.br/j/pn/a/VDmtz96hS9yVTyrtdh6DFMJ/?lang=en .
- **Flash safety.** W3C (2023), Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1 "Three Flashes or Below Threshold", https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html .
  - Nothing may flash more than 3 times in any 1 s, unless it stays below the general flash and red flash thresholds.
  - A general flash is a pair of opposing changes of ≥ 10% relative luminance, where the darker state is below 0.80.
  - A red flash is a pair of opposing transitions to or from saturated red, defined as R/(R+G+B) ≥ 0.8.
  - The safe-area limit is 0.006 sr, about 25% of any 10° field, or about 341×256 px at typical viewing distance.
  - Blinking faster than 3 per second also counts as flashing.
- Harding et al. (2005), "Photic- and pattern-induced seizures: expert consensus of the Epilepsy Foundation of America Working Group", *Epilepsia* 46(9), 1423–1425. A flash is a hazard at ≥ 20 cd/m², ≥ 3 Hz and ≥ 0.006 sr.
- Fisher et al. (2022), "Visually sensitive seizures: An updated review by the Epilepsy Foundation", *Epilepsia*, https://repository.essex.ac.uk/34275/ . Red flicker and alternating red and blue are the most provocative.
- **Music visualization reviews.**
  - Lima, Dos Santos and Meiguins (2021), "A Survey of Music Visualization Techniques", *ACM Computing Surveys* 54(7), https://doi.org/10.1145/3461835 .
  - Khulusi et al. (2020), "A Survey on Visualizations for Musical Data", *Computer Graphics Forum*, https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf13905 .
  - Both describe feature-to-visual mappings. I did not find in them a validated emotion-to-palette standard.

### Inference

- Make the wheel's step tick, a rotation snap with an impact point, the main beat cue. Use luminance pulses as seasoning.
- When audio latency is uncertain, let the visual land slightly early rather than late, since the window is wider when vision leads.
- A radial bloom outward on a downbeat draws the eye. Use it for accents, not every beat, so it keeps its power.
- Contraction inward reads calmer, which suits heartbreak and trance.
- The repo's music clock is 115 BPM (`src/music.js`), about 1.9 beats per second.
  - A full-field pulse on every beat is within 3/s.
  - A pulse on every eighth note (about 3.8/s) breaks 2.3.1 unless each pulse changes luminance by less than 0.10 or covers less than 25% of a 10° field.
- The existing `parada` strobe (`STROBE_ALPHA` 0.09 of bone, L ≈ 0.86, over void) changes luminance by about 0.077 at peak, so it is probably below threshold on its own.
  - Stacking it with other layers was not measured.

## 5. Palette and animation rules for Batuque

### Global rules

1. **Feeling lives in saturation and brightness, culture lives in hue.** Source: Valdez & Mehrabian 1994; Wilms & Oberfeld 2018.
   - Arousal is driven by saturation, plus red hue.
   - Valence is driven by brightness and saturation.
   - Dominance and weight come from a dark field with saturated accents.
2. **The tempo and mode axis** (Palmer et al. 2013):
   - fast and happy → lighter, saturated, yellower;
   - slow and sad → darker, desaturated, bluer.
3. **Timbre to lightness and shape** (Reymore & Lindsey 2025; Adeli et al. 2014; Spence 2011):
   - low and heavy voices → dark, large, slow shapes;
   - bright and high voices → light, small accents;
   - harsh voices → jagged edges.
4. **Motion per tempo** (inference from Sievers et al. 2013; Iversen et al. 2015):
   - One wheel step, a snap with an impact point, per beat.
   - Background rotation is a whole fraction of the beat: 1 turn per 4, 8 or 16 beats.
   - Jitter rises with fury. Step size rises with weight. Smooth easing goes with devotion and heartbreak.
5. **Sync:** schedule visual onsets 0–20 ms before the audio onset, never more than about 40 ms late. This is inference from van Wassenhove 2007 and Cecere 2016.
6. **Flash limits** (WCAG 2.3.1; Harding 2005):
   - Pulses that change luminance by ≥ 0.10: at most 3 per second over any area above 25% of a 10° field.
   - At or above 180 BPM, pulse on alternate beats.
   - Keep every red at R/(R+G+B) < 0.8. All values below are 0.72 or less.
   - Never alternate red and blue at speed.
   - Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`: stop pulses and halve rotation.
7. **Radial use** (Franconeri & Simons 2003):
   - an outward bloom only on phrase accents, at most 1 per bar;
   - an inward drift for calm sections.

### Per-rhythm palettes

Format: ground / body / accent / hot / light, in hex. "rr" is R/(R+G+B).

1. **Coco da Xambá: trance and devotion.** `#140b08` `#5a3523` `#9e2b25` `#c7793a` `#efe6d6`
   - Medium saturation (43–62%) and low to mid lightness. This gives arousal without euphoria (Valdez & Mehrabian).
   - Earth comes from the beaten-earth floor, white from initiates' cloth, both confirmed (Silva 2022).
   - The wine red is the folk Iansã red, unconfirmed for Xambá.
   - Motion: steady rotation, zero jitter, no flashes, a slow inward pull. The trance comes from repetition, not intensity.
   - Option: cycle many-coloured skirt accents at low saturation, after the 1949 "várias cores" description.
2. **Brega funk: sensuality and provocation.** `#0d0612` `#ff2e88` `#b81fd6` `#ff7a3d` `#ffd23f`
   - Full saturation on a near-black field gives high arousal with positive valence (Wilms & Oberfeld).
   - The pink-red family carries the love side of red (Jonauskaite et al. 2020).
   - The hues are inference. No brega funk colour study exists.
   - Motion: swinging, hip-like easing on the wheel. Sub-bass is a large dark bloom (Reymore & Lindsey), not a flash.
3. **Manguebeat: anger and weight.** `#0b0a07` `#3b2f22` `#b3261e` (rr 0.72) `#4f6b3a` `#d9d2b8`
   - A dark field with saturated rust gives dominance and arousal (Valdez & Mehrabian). Red carries anger (Jonauskaite).
   - Mud and mangrove follow the manifesto's imagery (Castro 2024). The pale signal colour stands for the antenna.
   - Motion: large, heavy steps with some jitter. The jagged edges follow the harsh-timbre mapping (Adeli 2014).
   - Option: a pixel-collage dither nods to the *Da Lama ao Caos* cover.
4. **Forró pé-de-serra: saudade inside the party.** `#0e0f1f` `#e08a2e` `#f2c14e` `#c4473a` `#3f7f8c` `#e9dcc0`
   - Warm, fairly saturated fire colours for the party, on an indigo night with a faded teal for saudade.
   - This mixes Palmer's happy (yellow, light) and sad (blue, dark) poles.
   - The fogueira, bandeirinhas and cordel paper are folk or press motifs.
   - Motion: a lilting swing with small steps. Bandeirinha triangles radiate from the wheel.
5. **Brega romântico: heartbreak.** `#0a0b18` `#2b3566` `#8c3b5a` `#c98ba0` `#d8d4e8`
   - Desaturated (30–41%), darker and bluer, the slow and sad pole (Palmer et al. 2013).
   - Muted rose keeps the love meaning of red at low arousal.
   - The single cold spotlight is inference. Stage colours are unconfirmed.
   - Motion: slow, smooth, downward drift, with long trails from feedback persistence.
6. **Frevo: euphoria with fury.** `#0a1230` `#ffd400` `#e8312a` (rr 0.72) `#1fa463` `#2a6fdb` `#ffffff`
   - Maximum saturation and brightness gives the most arousal and valence. Yellow is joy and red is fury (Jonauskaite).
   - Hues follow the Pernambuco flag (confirmed). The umbrella link rests on one secondary source.
   - Motion: the fastest rotation, high jitter, sharp spikes, and umbrella-segment radial wedges.
   - Flash risk is highest here: yellow on navy is a luminance change of 0.68. Keep the rule 6 limits.
7. **Maracatu de baque virado (existing).** `#07060b` `#ffc53d` `#ff5a36` `#3fd7ff` `#ff4fa3`
   - Already high saturation on void, which fits its arousal. Its reds are at 0.64 or less, which is safe.
   - Keep it as the most "neon" reference, so the new palettes differ mainly in saturation and brightness.

### Open uncertainties

- Iansã's colour in the Xambá house is unconfirmed. Ask the Xambá community.
- No colour study exists for brega funk, brega romântico or the manguebeat palette.
- Jonauskaite's sample may not include Brazil.
- I did not retrieve Sievers et al.'s per-emotion feature values.
